HR Analytics: Employee Attrition Dashboard

A multi-page Power BI dashboard analyzing employee attrition patterns, department performance, and headcount trends to identify what's driving employees out the door.

Problem Statement

High employee turnover is expensive and disruptive. This project analyzes HR data to answer: Who is leaving, from which departments, and why? — with the goal of surfacing actionable drivers of attrition rather than just reporting a headline number.

Data Source

Kaggle HR Analytics dataset (employee demographics, department, salary, travel frequency, attrition status, and related fields).

Tools Used
Power BI — dashboard design and data modeling
Power Query — data cleaning and transformation
DAX — custom measures for attrition rate, headcount, and department-level KPIs
Key Insights
Younger employees are leaving at a disproportionately higher rate than older, more tenured staff — suggesting a retention gap early in the employee lifecycle.
The Sales department shows the highest concentration of attrition compared to other departments, making it the primary area of concern.
Frequent business travel is strongly associated with higher attrition — employees who traveled frequently left at noticeably higher rates than those who didn't.
Compensation is a compounding factor: employees in the Sales department were also among the lowest-paid in the company, layering a pay concern on top of the travel burden and likely accelerating turnover in that group.

Takeaway: Attrition isn't random — it's concentrated in a specific, identifiable segment (young, Sales, frequent travelers, lowest pay). This means retention efforts can be targeted rather than company-wide, making them cheaper and more effective.


What I'd Explore Next

Building a simple attrition risk score combining age, department, travel frequency, and salary band to flag at-risk employees before they leave, rather than only reporting attrition after the fact.
